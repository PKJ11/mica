Swiggy: why is my food late?

A food delivery platform wants its app to promise delivery times it can actually keep. Sort the work of the analytics team into the six phases of CRISP-DM.

The business problem

Every time you order on Swiggy, the app shows a promise such as "Arriving in 32 mins". That number matters. If food arrives much later than promised, customers get annoyed, cancel orders, call customer care and sometimes switch to another app.

Imagine the customer experience team in Bengaluru has noticed a pattern. On weekday evenings between 7 pm and 10 pm, and especially on rainy days, far more orders arrive later than the app promised. They ask the analytics team to fix the delivery time estimate.

Terminology Alert: delivery time estimate. The number of minutes the app tells a customer their order will take, from placing the order to receiving it at the door. Companies often call this the ETA, short for estimated time of arrival.

The six phases of CRISP-DM

Terminology Alert: CRISP-DM. Short for Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining. It is a widely used plan that breaks an analytics project into six phases, so a team moves from a business question to a working solution in an orderly way.
1

Business understandingWhat is the business goal, and how will we know we have succeeded?

2

Data understandingWhat data do we have, and what does it look like? Is it complete and correct?

3

Data preparationClean the data, fix errors and create the columns the model will need.

4

ModellingBuild one or more models that make predictions from the prepared data.

5

EvaluationTest the model on new data. Does it meet the business goal from phase 1?

6

DeploymentPut the model to work in the real business and keep checking it.

Coming later: real projects rarely move in a straight line from phase 1 to phase 6. Teams often go back to an earlier phase. You will see an example of this after you finish the mapping.

Going back a step

In phase 5 the team checked the model on last month's orders. Suppose it was accurate on normal evenings but still badly wrong on rainy evenings.

The team would not jump to deployment. It would go back to phase 3 and add more useful information, such as how heavy the rain was or how many delivery partners were online in that area. Then it would build the model again in phase 4 and test it again in phase 5.

This is why CRISP-DM is usually drawn as a cycle. Evaluation can send you back to data preparation, and even deployment can reveal new business questions that start the cycle again at phase 1.
