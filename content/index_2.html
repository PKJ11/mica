Amul: how much milk should go out tonight?

A dairy brand wants to send each route the right number of milk pouches every morning. Sort the work of the analytics team into the six phases of CRISP-DM.

The business problem

Every morning, Amul milk reaches shops before most people wake up. Fresh milk pouches are dispatched overnight from chilling plants to distributors, and from distributors to thousands of small shops.

Getting the quantity right is hard. Send too few pouches and shops run out by 9 am, so customers buy another brand that day. Send too many and unsold pouches come back, and fresh milk cannot be kept for long.

Imagine the supply chain team wants to plan tomorrow's dispatch for each delivery route using past sales instead of the distributor's rough guess.

Terminology Alert: demand forecasting. Using past sales and other information, such as the day of the week or a festival, to estimate how much of a product customers will buy in a coming period. Here the period is tomorrow and the product is milk pouches on one route.

The six phases of CRISP-DM

Terminology Alert: CRISP-DM. Short for Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining. It is a widely used plan that breaks an analytics project into six phases, so a team moves from a business question to a working solution in an orderly way.
1

Business understandingWhat is the business goal, and how will we know we have succeeded?

2

Data understandingWhat data do we have, and what does it look like? Is it complete and correct?

3

Data preparationClean the data, fix errors and create the columns the model will need.

4

ModellingBuild one or more models that make predictions from the prepared data.

5

EvaluationTest the model on new data. Does it meet the business goal from phase 1?

6

DeploymentPut the model to work in the real business and keep checking it.

Coming later: real projects rarely move in a straight line from phase 1 to phase 6. Teams often go back to an earlier phase. You will see an example of this after you finish the mapping.

Going back a step

In phase 5 the team tested the forecast on the last three months. Suppose it worked well on ordinary days but was badly short on the two days before Diwali and on Janmashtami.

The team would not deploy it as it stands. It would go back to phase 3 and add better information about festivals, such as how large the local festival is and how many days of buying it causes. Then it would build the model again in phase 4 and test it again in phase 5.

This is why CRISP-DM is usually drawn as a cycle. Evaluation can send you back to data preparation, and once the forecast is running, a new question from the business can start the cycle again at phase 1.
